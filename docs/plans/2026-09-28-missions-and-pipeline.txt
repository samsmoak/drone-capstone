MISSIONS AND THE DATA PIPELINE — the 100 stages
===============================================

STATUS: IN PROGRESS (branch feat/missions-and-pipeline, off main at 733ba2a).
Written from the planning sessions of 2026-09-28 and checked against the code.
The handoff tickets for Hannah, Kevin and Reagan are in ../handoffs/ and build
against the skeleton this plan lands.

Two things are being built:

  MISSIONS   the operator draws a floor plan, places inspection points on it,
             saves it as a mission, and the drone flies it on its own.
  PIPELINE   every flight's readings and frames go through clean → enhance →
             classify → interpret, and every inspection point gets a verdict.

And the desktop Control page is remodelled for Auto, so that planning and
flying a mission is what the page is for.


THE WORDS, USED THE SAME WAY EVERYWHERE

  Room               the floor plan of one real space: a closed geofence, the
                     obstacles inside it, and (once measured) the Lighthouse
                     coverage around it. Saved once, reused by many missions.
  Inspection point   one place the drone must hold and record: x, y, height,
                     how long to hold, and a stable id ("P1").
  Mission            a room + a home mark + an ordered list of inspection
                     points + a cruise height. A plan; data only.
  Mission controller the part of the agent that takes a saved mission and
                     flies it, by giving goals to the manual flight system.
  Manual flight      ManualController (flight/manual.py): the tuned 50 Hz
  system             loop, its leash, its guards and its dead-man. The only
                     thing that ever commands the drone in Manual or in Auto.
  Pipeline           clean → enhance → classify → interpret, one inspection
                     point at a time.

DECISIONS

Missions
  - A room's geofence is ALWAYS CLOSED: a rectangle, a circle (stored as a
    32-sided polygon, so there is one shape to check), or at least 3 points,
    closed automatically, with no crossing edges and a non-zero area.
  - Layers nest: Lighthouse coverage ⊃ geofence ⊃ obstacles and path. Every
    inspection point and every LEG (home → P1 → … → home) is checked.
  - CLEARANCE is 0.25 m by default, per room, from every obstacle and from the
    geofence's own edge. Derived, not guessed: the drone's half-span (~0.05 m)
    + the manual system's drift notice (DRIFT_NOTICE_M, 0.15 m) + the
    estimator's measured noise (~0.05 m). A point on the fence line would be
    landed by the in-flight guard on ordinary drift.
  - Coverage is MEASURED by a hand-carried survey, never computed. Until a
    room has one, validation uses the agent's default fence (±2.0 m) as the
    outer bound and says "coverage not measured".
  - Every mission has a HOME mark: where the drone must sit before takeoff.
    Legs are checked from home, and the session refuses to start a mission
    when the drone is more than 0.30 m (the leash) from it.
  - Inspection points hold at least 5 s (story 3.4).
  - The mission controller flies a mission ONLY through
    ManualController.fly_to() and hold_at(). One way of flying, the tuned one.
    flight/missions.py and its go_to path are deleted.
  - Keys always win: any key held mid-mission cancels the goal and sets
    operator_override; the mission ends INTERRUPTED and the operator has the
    drone. The heartbeat dead-man stays with the app.
  - Rooms and missions are JSON on the laptop. Saving a mission that has
    flown makes a new REVISION; the flight keeps a copy of the exact plan
    flown in its session folder.
  - The mission controller itself is Hannah's (handoffs). This build lands
    its interface and a skeleton that REFUSES TO FLY (BUILT = False), and the
    session checks that BEFORE arming anything.
  - Not in this build: the base-station geometry fingerprint on a room (the
    agent has no reader for it yet) and the coverage survey tool (needs the
    lab).

Pipeline
  - Runs on the laptop, inside the agent. Built as ports and adapters so a
    cloud worker is a new adapter. Supabase stores results later; it does not
    compute (Deno only, 2 s of CPU per request).
  - The unit of work is ONE INSPECTION POINT. The same stage code serves live
    and batch runs.
  - The contract is ../handoffs/dpp-contract.txt → pipeline/contracts.py.
  - The batch runner runs in the CLI's own process. The live runner (a
    separate process, never a thread beside the 50 Hz loop) is a later stage.

Telemetry
  - point_id is stamped into every telemetry row and frames.csv row while the
    mission controller is HOLDING, and is empty otherwise.
  - The upload sends point_id only when a batch carries one. A flight with no
    mission uploads exactly as today, so nothing breaks before the Supabase
    migration is applied.

Desktop, Auto mode (Manual is unchanged)
  - Left: the Mission flow, three steps like the Zoomaa booking flow —
    ① Mission (list, view, edit, save, use), ② Check (the session and its
    checks), ③ Fly (Start mission at the top, the scene with the mission).
  - Right: FlightDeck (vitals, keys, action rail — never behind a tab), then
    the monitor tabs Vitals and Camera.
  - The draggable divider stays, with its own saved position for Auto.
  - The flow locks on Fly while a mission is flying.
