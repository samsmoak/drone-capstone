WHAT CHANGED SINCE THIS TICKET WAS WRITTEN

  The camera DOES deliver whole frames now: 8 748 real 324×244 grayscale
  frames on the lab laptop. Measured on 600 of them, they are DARK (median
  brightness 61 of 255) and low in contrast — contrast is what hides detail,
  not resolution. So the stage is CLAHE (contrast-limited adaptive histogram
  equalisation), which moves pixel values tile by tile and never draws a
  pixel that was not there. It also measures each ORIGINAL frame's quality —
  sharpness, brightness, black and white clipping — and marks the frames
  nobody can read (a hand over the lens, out of focus) as unusable, with why.
  No colour: the frames hold none, and colourising would guess it.


WHAT IS BUILT

  stages/enhance/clahe.py         ClaheEnhancer (clahe@1), measure_quality
  tests/pipeline/test_enhance.py  the stage's own rules; conformance runs too
  ml/enhance-eval/evaluate.py     settings and limits measured on real frames
  ml/enhance-eval/RESULTS.txt     the table, the choice, the limits, the time

  Settings: clip limit 2.0, 4×4 tiles — the most contrast (×1.21) for the
  grain of OpenCV's default. Limits: sharpness ≥ 25, mean ≥ 15, ≤ 50 % of
  pixels crushed or blown; 2.2 % of real frames fall outside them.
  Output: ctx.workdir/enhanced/<seq>.png, 8-bit grayscale, scale 1.
