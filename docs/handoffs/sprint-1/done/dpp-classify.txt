WHAT WAS BUILT, AND WHY IT IS NOT A TRAINED MODEL

  What the owner asked for (2026-10-09): find the BLOCKS of a flight where the
  temperature or pressure departs from what is normal — not every wiggle —
  say what happened there, and attach the frames taken then. The telemetry is
  the evidence; an image only supports it.

  The lab's flights showed that "normal" is not a constant. The barometer's
  own temperature COOLS from takeoff in the propellers' air (34.7 → 29.6 °C in
  48 s on a normal mission), and pressure falls 0.12 hPa per metre climbed. So
  blocks@1 compares each signal with what it should have read:

    temperature   raw_temp against a fitted first-order cooling curve
                  (robust: a heat event does not bend it)
    pressure      station pressure corrected for height by physics (ρ·g·h),
                  against its slow drift

  and splits the departure into blocks with PELT (Killick 2012, numpy;
  identical to the `ruptures` package on 500 of 500 test series). A block is
  an EVENT when it is beyond 4 × the flight's noise AND beyond the most any
  normal flight's blocks wander (0.8 °C, 0.2 hPa). Adjacent blocks departing
  the same way are one event (a warm patch comes out as a staircase).

  Measured on the lab laptop's flights (ml/anomaly-eval/MEASUREMENTS.txt):
  no temperature event on any of 115 normal flights; a planted warm patch of
  2 °C or more found every time, 1 °C in 97 %; a pressure step of 0.3 hPa
  found every time.
