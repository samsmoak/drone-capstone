FLIGHT SAFETY — the checks before a flight and the guards during one
====================================================================

WHAT IT IS

Nothing arms until a stepwise check run passes, and nothing stays in the air
once the drone's own data says it should not be. Both halves read the same
10 Hz telemetry stream (see telemetry-stream.txt), so a guard and the flight
recorder can never disagree about what happened.

This exists because of a crash on 2026-09-16: the drone took off, flew right,
and hit a wall. It had no position at all. See INVARIANTS below.


WHERE IT LIVES

  backend/agent/cropwatcher/flight/checks.py      the pre-flight steps, the tests
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/flight/supervisor.py  motors held after a crash
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/safety/flight_guard.py the in-flight guards
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/flight/control.py     GuardedFlight, FlightAborted
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/flight/programs.py    HoverTest
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/flight/geometry.py    measuring the station poses
  backend/agent/cropwatcher/cli.py                stations, geometry
  backend/agent/tests/test_checks.py, test_flight_guard.py, test_geometry.py


THE CHECKS (in order, each yields a CheckResult the UI renders live)

  radio        the drone answered on the radio; its hardware id is read from
               cpu.id0..2 as "cf-<hex>" and is how a flight ties to a drone
  telemetry    the 10 Hz stream is flowing
  motors       supervisor.info: crashed -> ask the firmware to recover and
               wait for it; tumbled -> stand it up; locked -> restart it
  battery      sys.canfly is the authority, not a voltage this code invented;
               the voltage is shown for the operator, never used as the gate
  positioning  base stations RECEIVED and calibrated and with geometry.
               ONE is enough to fly (MIN_USABLE_STATIONS = 1); a room with two
               insists on both with CROPWATCHER_MIN_STATIONS=2. Zero is not a
               looser setting — it is unassisted flight, offered by name.
  still        the Kalman estimate has settled in x, y AND z

On request, once the area is confirmed: the BATTERY & MOTOR TEST — the
firmware's propeller test (each motor alone, health.motorPass) then its battery
test (all four under load, health.batterySag / batteryPass). Both verdicts are
the firmware's own; the agent records batTestPWMRatio beside the result.

Only three results BLOCK a session: no telemetry, the firmware refusing to
arm (sys.canfly = 0), and motors the supervisor will not release. Deck, base stations and settling WARN instead — the person
standing over the drone decides whether to fly (decided 2026-09-16). A warning
returns the report with assisted = False, which means:

  - no preset programs (Hover test is refused: it would fly blind)
  - manual flight is UNASSISTED: W/S move the throttle, not a height
  - the position guards are off (see below); tumble, battery, stale telemetry
    and the heartbeat still run
  - confirming needs a second, explicit acknowledgement

Manual mode runs exactly the same checks as auto — the risk is identical.


THE GUARDS (checked on every sample while flying)

  TUMBLED          supervisor bit 5            -> STOP
  POSITION_LOST    usable stations < 2         -> STOP
  RECEPTION_LOST   no reception past 0.5 s     -> LAND
  BATTERY_LOW      pm.state == 3 (lowPower)    -> LAND
  DRIFT            > 0.30 m from the target    -> LAND
  HEIGHT_ERROR     > 0.25 m from commanded     -> LAND
  OUTSIDE_FENCE    beyond the takeoff fence    -> LAND
  TELEMETRY_STALE  the stream stopped          -> LAND

Unassisted (GuardContext.assisted = False) only TUMBLED, BATTERY_LOW and
TELEMETRY_STALE apply. Every other guard reads a position, and without base
stations that "position" is the accelerometer integrating — they would fire on
noise and steer a landing by nothing.

An abort raises FlightAborted(verdict); the reason is stored on the flight row
(flights.abort_reason) and written to the audit trail.


WATCHING THE BASE STATIONS FROM THE LAB

  cropwatcher stations          live, once a second, until Ctrl-C

Prints what the DRONE reports — received, calibrated, geometry, its estimate
and the verdict — while someone powers or re-aims a station. A station's own
LED says it is running; it does not say the drone can see it, and the second
station is the difference between a drone that holds position and one that
hops sideways. Reads only: no arming, no motors.

Stored geometry is not signal, again. A station can show under calibrated and
geometry — it was seen once, from that position — while not being received at
all. That was the lab's state on 2026-09-21: received 0, calibrated 0 and 1.


MEASURING WHERE THE STATIONS ARE

  cropwatcher geometry                  walks the steps and writes the result
  cropwatcher geometry --distance 1.25  the measured distance that sets scale
  cropwatcher geometry --dry-run        solve and print, write nothing

The drone finds its position by crossing the beams of two stations, so it has
to know where each station IS. That is the geometry, and it is stored ON THE
DRONE, surviving a power cycle. It goes stale the moment a station is nudged,
re-aimed, or the drone is flown in another room.

Stale geometry does not fail loudly. The stations simply disagree and the
estimate jumps between their answers, with every indicator still green.
Measured on 2026-09-21 with both stations received 96 % of the time and the
drone SITTING STILL on the floor: the position jumped 21 cm in a single 0.1 s
step and wandered 33 cm in ten seconds. Nothing downstream can recover from
that — the height hold, the geofence, the drift guard and the held spot in
manual flight all read a position that is lying.

The procedure is Bitcraze's own (the cflib example
lighthouse/multi_bs_geometry_estimation.py, which is what cfclient runs),
driven from our CLI so a lab session needs no cfclient. The drone is CARRIED
by hand for every sample and no motor ever spins:

  1. at the ORIGIN        where (0, 0, 0) should be, facing "forward"
  2. on the +X AXIS       a measured distance away — this sets the scale
  3. 4x in the XY PLANE   spread around the floor, corners are good
  4. 6x around the SPACE  including up in the air, where it actually flies

Every position needs BOTH stations. One station reads fine and poisons the
solution, so a single-station sample is refused by name rather than accepted.

WHEN THERE IS ONLY ONE STATION

Lighthouse V2 positions from a single station — one unit sweeps two angles and
the deck's four sensors resolve a pose from them. Measured 2026-09-22 with one
station and the drone at rest: position held to sub-millimetre, varPX 0.0001 m²
(1 cm). So a room with one station is not a room with none.

The solver above cannot do it: it works by crossing the views of station PAIRS,
and one station is not a pair. `cropwatcher geometry` notices what is actually
being RECEIVED and switches to IPPE instead — the four photodiodes are a known
planar target, and the angles fix where the station must be.

IPPE returns TWO poses for a planar target, and for one 3 cm across seen from
metres away they reproject almost identically. The wrong one is a MIRROR: a
room where forward is backward. Reprojection error alone picks it wrong often
enough to matter, so the second sample, a measured distance forward, decides —
only the true pose puts the station in the same place seen from both. They must
agree within SINGLE_STATION_AGREEMENT_M (0.25 m) or the answer is refused.

It costs redundancy that no tuning recovers. One viewpoint means an occluded
deck has no second opinion, and this drone carries NO flow deck and NO
rangefinder to coast on (measured: deck.bcFlow2 = 0, deck.bcZRanger2 = 0).
Lose the one station and x and y are the accelerometer integrating — measured
diverging at about 1 m/s the same day. So the checks still want two, and one
is accepted only by asking, per room: CROPWATCHER_MIN_STATIONS=1.


DECISIONS

- Lost position means STOP, not land. A landing steers by a position estimate;
  with no position there is nothing to steer by, so cutting is the safer end.
  Every other fault lands gracefully.
- Battery low uses the firmware's own pm.state, not a voltage threshold. A
  guessed 3.70 V refused flights the drone would have allowed. The firmware's
  low point is pm.lowVoltage (Bitcraze default 3.2 V; critical 3.0 V). The
  agent's own 3.10 V backstop, held past a 1 s grace, sits BELOW that — it only
  catches a firmware that never reports. Lowering either buys seconds: under
  hover load a 1S LiPo collapses fast below ~3.2 V, and the unassisted landing
  needs about four. The juice that was actually lost went to the bang-bang
  thrust: a full-throttle slam sagged 4.14 V to 3.57 V in 100 ms (2026-09-17).
- An abnormal end requires a Retry that re-runs the checks (sessions-and-
  modes.txt). The motors check is what makes the retry mean something.
- "Cut motors" as an operator control was removed. The normal control is Land
  (graceful); Emergency stop is hold-for-one-second and has no key binding.
- The guard verdict is data (Action + Reason enums), not a callback. The same
  verdict drives the abort, the audit event and the message on screen.
- Geometry is re-measured, not adjusted. There is no way to tell a drifting
  estimate from a moved station by watching the numbers, so the fix is to walk
  the samples again rather than nudge a stored pose.
- The geometry walk takes the samples through a caller-supplied callback, so
  the order, the refusals and the wording are tested without a drone or a
  person in the room.


INVARIANTS

- Advisory is not the same as trusted. A positioning warning must always
  return assisted = False. The 2026-09-16 crash was a blind drone told it could
  hold a position; tests pin that the crash readings come back unassisted.
- Lost position still outranks low battery in an assisted flight: landing steers
  by the estimate, so with no estimate the motors stop instead.
- Losing the DOWNLINK is not losing the drone. Telemetry going quiet means we
  are BLIND, not that the drone is in danger: setpoints still go up at 50 Hz
  and, if the uplink is alive, it is still flying them. A gap is ridden out to
  STALE_TELEMETRY_S + STALE_TELEMETRY_GRACE_S (2.0 s) and the snapshot's own
  AGE is the evidence, so a very old first sample lands at once rather than
  starting a fresh grace period. While blind, no other guard speaks — a stale
  snapshot cannot say anything true about now, so reading a tumble or a
  position out of it would be inventing one. Before this there was no grace at
  all: one late sample ended a flight on 2026-09-22.

- A TOTAL RADIO LOSS DROPS THE DRONE and nothing here can change that. The
  firmware detects a dead link at about 1 s and CUTS THE MOTORS at about 2 s;
  it does not glide. What does help is that landing hands to the HIGH-LEVEL
  COMMANDER, which runs the descent on the drone — so once that command
  arrives, the landing finishes with no further radio. A lost downlink lands
  gently; a lost uplink cannot be helped, because the command cannot arrive.

- Losing the BEAMS is not losing the POSITION. The Kalman coasts on the IMU
  and publishes how well it is coasting: its variance GROWS while it has no
  measurement to correct with. So a blackout is ridden out for up to
  RECEPTION_BLACKOUT_MAX_S (3.0 s) while the variance stays under
  RECEPTION_COAST_VARIANCE_M2 (0.04 m², 20 cm), and ended inside
  RECEPTION_LOST_GRACE_S (0.5 s) when it does not. A flat half-second landed
  a drone on 2026-09-22 that was holding x and y to 1.7 cm, with the beams
  back 0.2 s later. With ONE station a brief occlusion is ordinary — the
  airframe's own tilt can break the line — so reception alone was never a
  safe thing to abort on.

- Re-measure the geometry after ANY station moves, and before trusting a held
  position. Everything that reads a position inherits its error, silently.
- Choose the method from what is RECEIVED, never from what is stored.
  bsCalVal and bsGeoVal listed both stations on 2026-09-22 while only one was
  reaching the drone: that is last session's memory, and picking the pair
  solver on the strength of it would have hung waiting for a second station
  that was not there.
- lighthouse.bsAvailable is STORED geometry, not received signal. On the day of
  the crash it read 4 stations while bsReceive read 0: the preflight passed on
  stored data and the drone flew blind. Count only stations that are received
  AND calibrated (bsCalVal) AND have geometry (bsGeoVal), and require at least
  two. This one line is the whole reason the drone hit a wall.
- Settle on x, y and z. The old check watched z alone, so a wandering horizontal
  estimate passed as "still". Require the spread across the window under about
  2 cm on all three.
- Never sleep a fixed time after a Kalman reset. The estimate keeps converging
  for seconds; sampling early put a ground reference 0.14 m wrong.
- Lighthouse z = 0 is not the floor. Measured floor level ranged 0.85–1.40 m in
  the same room across runs. Capture ground at takeoff; commanded height is
  relative to it. The agent stores ground-relative z — do not subtract it again
  when reading telemetry back.
- Read sys.canfly before commanding flight. The supervisor refuses to arm below
  roughly 3.75 V and reports this itself.
- After a tumble the supervisor holds every motor at zero until told to
  recover. On 2026-09-17 the flight after a tumble reported "flying" for 19 s
  with thrust 0 and all four motors at 0. Never arm without reading
  supervisor.info first; the checks do, and a retry runs the checks.
- Call send_notify_setpoint_stop() before the high-level commander. Any earlier
  low-level setpoint in the same power cycle holds priority and silently locks
  the high-level commander out: takeoff() returns success with motors at 0.
