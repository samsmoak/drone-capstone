HOW THIS FILE WORKS

Each update is a COMPLETE, SELF-CONTAINED statement of where flight stands —
not a diff against the one before it. Read the top one and you know the state
of the system; you never have to reconstruct it by reading downwards.

Nothing is ever deleted. Older updates stay below, in full, separated by a
rule, so the history of what we believed and when is recoverable. Where a
later update CONTRADICTS an earlier one, the later one says so explicitly.

This applies to flight only. `flight-log.txt` is the raw session journal —
readings as they were taken. This file is what those readings mean.

UPDATE 5 — 2026-09-22, late evening
========================================================================

HEADLINE

The control law is not the problem and has not changed. The last five flights
were all BAROMETER flights — drift_m reads 0.0000 on every row, so no
commanded point existed — with vertical swings of 0.67, 1.40 and 1.86 m. They
fell back to the barometer because THE DRONE HAS NO VALID GEOMETRY: it
receives base station 0 but cannot turn those beams into a position, and
reports the estimate uncertain by 525 cm. Geometry has still never been run.

Separately, two real controller faults were found and fixed, one of them
introduced by yesterday's own fix.


THE TWO CONTROLLER FAULTS

1. THE DRONE WAS LIVING ON MELLINGER. Measured on the bench: after a full day
   of flights that every one of them pinned stabilizer.controller to 1, the
   parameter read 2. The cause is restore(): it writes back exactly what it
   found, it found Mellinger, so the END of every flight handed Mellinger
   back, and the next flight started from it again. The leftover that the
   pinning exists to remove was being re-applied for ever.
   Mellinger reads NONE of the gains in flight/tuning.py and lurches without
   an excellent position estimate — the documented cause of the 2026-09-21
   crashes.
   FIXED: the controller is now LEFT ON PID. Nobody chose Mellinger, so there
   is no operator state to preserve; handing it back is a bug, not politeness.
   Everything else is still restored exactly.

2. THE RE-INIT COULD LEAVE THE DRONE ON THE BOUNCE. This one was introduced by
   the fix in update 4. Forcing controllerInit() requires writing a DIFFERENT
   controller type first, and the value chosen was 2 — Mellinger. So a silent
   failure of the second write would fly the drone on precisely the controller
   the profile exists to escape, and nothing checked.
   FIXED: the value is read back after the bounce and a mismatch raises rather
   than flying unverified. Being left on the bounce is worse than never
   bouncing at all.


WHY THE SMOOTHNESS WENT AWAY

Not the control law. The smooth flights — the ones called "buttery smooth" for
up and down and "marvellous" sideways — were ASSISTED, holding x and y to
under 2 cm with thrust steady near 56,000 and no saturation. Every flight
since has been unassisted, which is the 72 cm-of-noise sensor diagnosed in
update 3.

The tuning column in the trace names the law, and it is the first thing to
read before drawing any conclusion from a flight. A barometer profile
(velCtlPid.vzKp lowered) means nothing about the commanded point.


WHAT IS PROVEN ON HARDWARE

- The commanded point, and every axis not being pressed staying locked.
- Height holding when the keys are released.
- One base station settling to 1.3-1.8 cm — WHEN it has geometry.
- Crash recovery, honest refusal messages, motors, battery, deck detection.
- A connect that cannot hang, and a busy message that names the step.

WHAT IS FIXED BUT HAS NOT FLOWN

- Beam blackouts ridden out to 3 s on the filter's own variance.
- Telemetry gaps ridden out to 2.0 s.
- The controller rebuilt before every flight so a crash's wound-up
  integrators do not carry into the next takeoff.
- Mellinger never handed back; the bounce verified.
- IS_FLYING on the ground reported as a named warning.


WHAT IS NOT FIXED

1. GEOMETRY HAS NEVER RUN. It is now blocking, not merely advisable: without
   it the drone cannot produce a position at all, so every flight falls back
   to the barometer and cannot be smooth. It is also the cause of the rotated
   directions in update 4.
2. A TOTAL RADIO LOSS STILL DROPS THE DRONE. Firmware cuts motors at about
   2 s and does not glide. Only a lost downlink can be landed gently.
3. drift_m AND THE COMMANDED POINT ARE INVISIBLE to the operator.
4. FIRMWARE IS OLD — CRTP 10 against the 12 cflib wants. send_arming_request
   is silently a no-op because of it.
5. IS_FLYING CANNOT BE CLEARED from here. Reported, not fixable; a power cycle
   is the only reliable reset.


WHAT NEEDS REDIRECTION

- NOTHING ABOUT THE CONTROL LAW SHOULD BE JUDGED, TUNED OR CHANGED until a
  flight runs assisted again. Five barometer flights in a row produced five
  impressions of a law that never executed.
- RUN GEOMETRY BEFORE THE NEXT FLIGHT. Not as a step in a ladder — as the
  precondition for the system working at all.
- DO NOT FIX THE ROTATED DIRECTIONS BY FLIPPING THE KEYS. Still true, and
  still for the same reason: the frame is wrong, not the mapping.


THE ORDER TO DO IT IN

  1  ./cropwatcher geometry --distance 1.0   the precondition for everything
  2  ./cropwatcher check                     expect settle under 2 cm
  3  Fly ASSISTED. Confirm the app does NOT offer the barometer.
  4  Re-test directions, then a climb-and-release, then side steps.

UPDATE 4 — 2026-09-22, evening
========================================================================

HEADLINE

The control law works. It flew, twice, and the operator's words were
"buttery smooth" for up and down and "marvellous" for sideways, with the
height holding when the keys were released. Both flights ended early, and
NEITHER ended because of the control law: one on a half-second base-station
dropout, one on a half-second telemetry gap. Both of those guards have been
fixed. The one real outstanding fault is that DIRECTIONS ARE ROTATED, and the
cause is the one step of the ladder we skipped — geometry has still never
been measured.


WHAT THE TRACES MEASURED

Climb, keys held (trace_0f947214.csv):

  drift from the commanded point   0.0006 .. 0.017 m
  thrust                            steady near 56,000
  thrust above the actuator max     0%
  motors at zero while airborne     0%
  controller                        stabilizer.controller = 2 -> 1 (PID)

Against the barometer flight three hours earlier, same drone:

  thrust above the actuator max     46%   (peak 541,901 vs max 65,535)
  motors at zero while airborne     17%   — momentary free fall
  barometer z, target frozen        72 cm of swing

That is the whole argument for position control in two tables. The commanded
point held x and y to under 2 cm through an entire climb.


WHAT IS FIXED, AND PROVEN ON HARDWARE

- The commanded point. Every axis not being pressed is locked; the firmware
  corrects to it at 100 Hz. Flew, worked.
- Height holds when the keys are released. Confirmed by the operator.
- One base station is enough. Checks pass, position settles to 1.3-1.8 cm.
- Crash recovery. A latched IS_CRASHED cleared in under a second.
- Honest refusal messages. `sys.canfly = 0` names the real reason instead of
  blaming the battery — which had sent the operator to a charger holding a
  4.15 V cell, nearly full.
- One command builds the app AND the agent inside it. A stale sidecar is the
  reason every flight before 14:30 ran on the barometer.

WHAT IS FIXED, BUT HAS NOT FLOWN YET

- Beam blackouts are ridden out for up to 3 s while the Kalman's own variance
  stands behind the position, instead of a flat 0.5 s. The 0.5 s landed a
  drone that was holding x and y to 1.7 cm, with the beams back 0.2 s later.
- Telemetry gaps are ridden out to 2.0 s total. There was NO grace at all
  before — one late sample landed a flight, where every other guard requires
  a fault to persist.


WHAT IS NOT FIXED

1. DIRECTIONS ARE ROTATED. Forward moves backward, left moves right.
   CAUSE: not the key mapping and not the rotation maths. While the operator
   held `forward`, stabilizer.yaw read 164.7 deg — the drone believed it was
   facing almost exactly backwards. At that heading cos = -0.96, so "forward
   along the heading" correctly means decreasing x, and the trace shows
   target_x falling from 0.707 to 0.331 with the drone following to within
   1-12 cm. The loop is faithful; the FRAME is wrong.
   WHY: the world frame is defined by the stored base-station geometry, and
   that geometry has never been measured. It is left over from a previous
   session with the stations somewhere else. A rotated frame rotates yaw.
   FIX: run `cropwatcher geometry`. NOT a sign flip in code — inverting the
   keys would be a compensation for a wrong frame, and it would invert again
   the moment geometry is measured properly. See the note on masks below.

2. GEOMETRY HAS NEVER RUN AGAINST HARDWARE. Rung 2 of the ladder, still.
   Everything in 1 above follows from it.

3. A TOTAL RADIO LOSS STILL DROPS THE DRONE, and no agent change can alter
   that. Bitcraze's firmware detects a dead link at about 1 s and CUTS THE
   MOTORS at about 2 s. It does not glide. What does work: our landing hands
   to the HIGH-LEVEL COMMANDER, which runs the descent ON the drone — so once
   that command arrives, the landing completes without any further radio. So
   a lost DOWNLINK (we go blind, uplink alive) now lands gently; a lost
   UPLINK cannot be helped from here, because the command cannot arrive.

4. drift_m AND THE COMMANDED POINT ARE INVISIBLE to the operator. They are in
   the trace and the agent log only.

5. FIRMWARE IS OLD. CRTP protocol 10; cflib wants 12 and falls back to legacy
   supervisor handling.


WHAT NEEDS IMPROVEMENT

- ONE STATION HAS NO REDUNDANCY, and this drone has nothing to coast on —
  deck.bcFlow2 = 0 and deck.bcZRanger2 = 0, read off the drone. A second
  station is still the single biggest robustness win available, and it costs
  no code.
- THE BLACKOUT CEILINGS (3.0 s for beams, 2.0 s for telemetry) ARE REASONED,
  NOT MEASURED. kalman.varPX and varPY are now in the trace so the next real
  dropout can be judged on data rather than argument.
- CONTROLLER SATURATION IS STILL UNREPORTED. A controller demanding more than
  full throttle is a loud upstream signal and is currently visible only by
  reading a CSV afterwards.


WHAT NEEDS REDIRECTION

- DO NOT FIX THE DIRECTIONS BY FLIPPING THE KEYS. It is the reason this
  section exists. The frame is wrong, not the mapping, and a compensation
  here is inherited by every autonomous flight — see below.
- DO NOT TUNE HEIGHT GAINS. Established in update 3 and still true: the
  barometer flight's fault was a lying sensor against a frozen command.
- JUDGE THE CONTROL LAW ONLY FROM ASSISTED FLIGHTS. The tuning column names
  the law. A barometer profile means nothing about the commanded point.


MANUAL IS THE FOUNDATION FOR AUTONOMOUS

The commanded point is not a manual-flight trick: a waypoint mission IS the
same machinery with a different source of intent. Manual moves the point with
keys; autonomous moves it along a plan. Same seeding, same leash, same
estimator gates, same guards, same trace.

So anything hacked in here to paper over a sensor or a frame fault is
inherited by every autonomous flight, where nobody is watching to correct it.
That is the whole argument against flipping the keys in 1 above, and against
tuning height gains in update 3. The quality bar for manual IS the quality
bar for the system.


THE ORDER TO DO IT IN

  1  cropwatcher geometry --distance 1.0    fixes the rotated frame
  2  cropwatcher check                      expect settle under 2 cm
  3  Re-test directions. Forward should go where the nose points.
       If it is STILL inverted after correct geometry, it is a genuine sign
       bug and the yaw convention needs pinning — sit the drone facing a
       known way, read stabilizer.yaw, rotate it 90 deg left, read again.
  4  Re-test a climb-and-release with the new blackout tolerances.
  5  Side steps, then a square.

Last updated from hardware: 2026-09-22.


WHERE WE ARE IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Sideways control is better and the operator confirms it. Height is not: let
go after a climb and the drone sinks and rises continually. The cause is
measured and it is not the control law — all three flights on 2026-09-22 ran
UNASSISTED, holding height on the BAROMETER, which wandered 72 cm while our
height target sat frozen. The position work built in PRs #25 and #26 has
never run once. The single action that matters most is rebuilding the desktop
sidecar so the app can fly assisted on the one base station the cage has.


THE MEASUREMENT THAT EXPLAINS THE HEIGHT PROBLEM

14.7 s of trace_cc73c0ef.csv, hands off the keys, drone in the air:

  our height target      0.371 m       FROZEN for the whole 14.7 s
  barometer z            -0.07..+0.65  a 72 cm range
  motors                 0 .. 65535    repeatedly, full off to full on

The agent commanded one steady height and never moved it. The barometer
reported the drone swinging through 72 cm. The firmware believed the
barometer, saw a 50 cm error that did not exist, and answered with everything
it had — then cut to zero. The drone physically porpoised chasing sensor noise.

For comparison, the SAME DRONE on the Lighthouse, one base station, an hour
earlier: steady to 1.3 cm. That is 55x better. The fix for height is not
gains; it is flying on the sensor that works.

(The docs previously said an indoor barometer wanders "tens of centimetres".
Measured here: 72 cm. Worse than written.)


WHY EACH REPORTED BEHAVIOUR HAPPENS

Symptom, cause, the evidence, and whether it is fixed. Nothing here is
inferred from how quadrotors usually behave; each cause is a measurement.

1. "IT HOVERS BUT LOWERS AND RISES CONTINUALLY, AND WILL NOT LOCK A HEIGHT"
   CAUSE    The barometer, not the control law.
   EVIDENCE Our height target sat frozen at 0.371 m for 14.7 s while the
            barometer reported a 72 cm swing. The firmware believed it.
            Across that flight the controller demanded MORE THAN FULL
            THROTTLE in 46% of samples (peak 541,901 against an actuator
            maximum of 65,535 — eight times over) and NEGATIVE thrust in
            12%. Motors sat at exactly 0 for 17% of airborne samples.
   MEANING  The controller spent 58% of the flight demanding something
            physically impossible, because it was correcting an error that
            did not exist. The porpoising is the airframe following a
            fictional altitude. Motors at zero in the air is free fall.
   FIXED?   NO — but the cause is not in our code. Fly assisted and z comes
            from the Lighthouse, measured at 1.3 cm against the barometer's
            72 cm.

2. "SIDEWAYS IS A LITTLE BETTER TODAY"
   CAUSE    The easing, not the axis locking.
   EVIDENCE The flight ran the barometer law, where the arrows tilt the
            drone. What improved it is the eased tilt and yaw rates
            (TILT_RATE_DEG_S, YAW_ACCEL_DEG_S2) from the earlier glide work.
   MEANING  A real improvement, and worth keeping — but NOT evidence about
            PR #26. The commanded point never ran. Judgement on the new law
            has to wait for an assisted flight.
   FIXED?   Partly, and by something other than what was assumed.

3. "IT GOES SIDEWAYS, HOPS, AND CRASHES" (the original complaint)
   CAUSE    Three, compounding.
            (a) Velocity-only control. A speed never says WHERE, so tilt
                error integrated into position with nothing observing
                position. A quadrotor is underactuated — it moves sideways
                by tilting — so one degree of unnoticed tilt is 0.17 m/s^2,
                34 cm of drift in two seconds.
            (b) stabilizer.controller = 2 (Mellinger), which ignores every
                gain in flight/tuning.py.
            (c) Stale geometry: 21 cm estimate jumps with the drone still.
   FIXED?   (a) shipped, never flown. (b) pinned to PID before every flight.
            (c) tool written, never run against hardware.

4. "FLIGHTS LAST SECONDS; MY TEAM GOT NINE MINUTES"
   CAUSE    Tumbles, not battery. Session history: aborted-tumbled at 11.6,
            11.1, 15.7, 20.3 and 8.2 seconds.
   FIXED?   Addressed at the root by 3 above, not yet demonstrated.

5. "IT WILL NOT ARM AT 4.15 V"
   CAUSE    A latched crash. supervisor.info = 644, IS_CRASHED set. Battery
            4.13 V, pm.state 0 (normal), roll and pitch under one degree.
            Nothing to do with power.
   FIXED?   YES. One recovery request cleared it; the message now names the
            real reason.

6. "THE DESKTOP SAYS I NEED TWO BASE STATIONS"
   CAUSE    The app runs a FROZEN copy of the agent, built before the change.
   FIXED?   In the agent, yes. In the app, only after a rebuild.

7. "IT FLEW UP BEAUTIFULLY, THEN FELL STRAIGHT DOWN WHEN I LET GO"
   CAUSE    Not the release, and not a fall. The base station dropped out for
            about half a second at that moment and the guard landed it.
   EVIDENCE trace_0f947214.csv. The climb held x and y to between 0.0006 and
            0.017 m, thrust steady near 56,000, no saturation — the first
            flight of the commanded point, and it worked. At t=5.1 s
            lighthouse.bsReceive went to 0 and the key was released; at 5.5 s
            the state went to landing; at 5.7 s the beams were BACK and
            stayed. Descent 1.03 m to 0.37 m over 2.1 s, about 0.31 m/s,
            motors steady near 50,000 — a controlled landing, not a drop. The
            operator pressed up at 7.8 s and nothing happened: landing
            ignores the keys.
   MEANING  The guard aborted on RECEPTION, which is a proxy. What matters is
            whether the ESTIMATE is still good, and it was.
   FIXED?   YES. A blackout is now ridden out while the filter's own variance
            stands behind its position (up to 3 s), and ended inside 0.5 s
            when the variance is growing instead.


MANUAL IS THE FOUNDATION FOR AUTONOMOUS

This is why the architecture matters more than the symptom list. The
commanded point is not a manual-flight trick: a waypoint mission IS the same
machinery with a different source of intent. Manual moves the point with
keys; autonomous moves it along a plan. Same seeding, same leash, same
estimator gates, same guards, same trace.

Two consequences worth holding on to:

- Anything hacked into the manual law to paper over a sensor fault will be
  inherited by every autonomous flight, where nobody is watching to correct
  it. That is the strongest argument against tuning height gains to mask
  barometer noise.

- The quality bar for manual is therefore the quality bar for the whole
  system. Getting a clean, measured hover is not a milestone on the way to
  the real work; it IS the real work, and the mission layer is a thin thing
  on top of it.


WHAT IS DONE — and whether it has actually flown

  PROVEN ON HARDWARE
  - One base station accepted, and it is enough. Checks pass, position
    settles to 1.3 cm. PR #27.
  - Crash recovery. The drone had latched IS_CRASHED; one recovery request
    cleared it in under a second.
  - Honest refusal messages. `sys.canfly = 0` no longer reads as "flat
    battery" — it names what the firmware says. This cost a lab hour before
    it was fixed: a 4.15 V cell, nearly full, sent to the charger.
  - The estimator's own confidence is readable and real: varPX 0.0001 m^2,
    about 1 cm.
  - Motors, battery, telemetry, deck detection.

  SHIPPED, NEVER FLOWN — this is the honest gap
  - The commanded point: every axis you are not pressing is locked. PR #26.
  - Auto-correction to that point, continuously, moving or still.
  - Multi-key rules: opposite pairs cancel and HOLD; different axes compose.
  - The 30 cm leash.
  - Estimator-jump rejection at 2 m/s, and the 10 cm variance gate.
  - Single-station geometry with mirror disambiguation. PR #27.
  All of it is tested — 469 tests — and none of it has met a drone.


WHAT IS NOT FIXED

1. HEIGHT DOES NOT HOLD.   Cause measured (above): barometer noise, not the
   control law. Not yet retested on the Lighthouse, which is the actual fix.

2. THE DESKTOP APP RUNS STALE CODE.  It bundles a FROZEN copy of the agent
   (`externalBin`), built before today. That copy still demands two base
   stations, so the app refuses assisted flight, offers "fly on the
   barometer", and the operator takes it — which is how every flight today
   ended up on the worst available sensor. THIS IS THE ROOT CAUSE OF THE
   HEIGHT PROBLEM, one level up.

3. GEOMETRY HAS NEVER RUN AGAINST HARDWARE.  Until it does, directions may be
   rotated or mirrored: "forward" is not guaranteed to be where the nose
   points. Hover and up/down are unaffected. Arrow tests are not meaningful.

4. drift_m AND THE COMMANDED POINT ARE INVISIBLE.  They go to the trace CSV
   and the agent log. Nothing shows the operator that the drone is 15 cm off
   the spot and correcting.

5. FIRMWARE IS OLD.  CRTP protocol 10; cflib wants 12 and falls back to
   legacy supervisor handling. Not blocking, but a standing suspect.


WHAT NEEDS IMPROVEMENT

- ONE STATION HAS NO REDUNDANCY. It works, and it is measurably good, but a
  single viewpoint means an occluded deck has nowhere else to look — and this
  drone carries no flow deck and no rangefinder (deck.bcFlow2 = 0,
  deck.bcZRanger2 = 0, read off the drone). Lose sight of the station and x
  and y become the accelerometer integrating, measured diverging at about
  1 m/s to 18 m in seconds. A second station is the single biggest robustness
  win available and costs no code.

- THE BAROMETER LAW SHOULD BE HARDER TO REACH BY ACCIDENT. Today it was the
  default outcome of a stale binary. It should be a deliberate choice that
  states what it costs, not the path of least resistance.

- MOTORS REACHING EXACTLY 0 IN FLIGHT is momentary free fall — 17% of
  airborne samples on 2026-09-22. A floor under commanded thrust while
  airborne is worth considering, but ONLY after the estimate is fixed.
  Applied now it would mask the real fault, and per the note above, a mask
  in the manual law is inherited by every autonomous flight.

- CONTROLLER SATURATION IS NOT MEASURED ANYWHERE. 46% of samples demanded
  above the actuator maximum and 12% demanded negative thrust, and nothing
  in the system noticed or said so. A controller spending most of a flight
  asking for the impossible is a loud signal that something upstream is
  wrong, and it is currently only visible by reading a CSV afterwards.


WHAT NEEDS REDIRECTION — where the instructions were wrong

- "TUNE THE HEIGHT GAINS." Wrong target. The trace shows a frozen command and
  a lying sensor. Tuning a controller against a bad estimate hides the fault
  and makes the next one harder to find. Fix the estimate first, then measure
  again before touching a gain.

- "THE BATTERY THRESHOLD IS TOO HIGH." It never was. Both times flight was
  refused this week the battery was healthy — 4.15 V on 2026-09-22, and a
  latched crash was the real reason. Read the reason, never assume it.

- "WE DO NOT NEED THE LIGHTHOUSE TO FLY." True, and it is exactly what went
  wrong today: flying without it is what produced the porpoising. The
  Lighthouse is not a nice-to-have on this airframe, it is the only sensor
  that measures position or a trustworthy height.

- TEST ASSISTED BEFORE JUDGING THE NEW CONTROL LAW. Every impression of the
  drone so far — including "sideways is better" — comes from the barometer
  law. The axis-locking work has not been seen yet, well or badly.
