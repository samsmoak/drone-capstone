FLIGHT STATUS — where the drone actually is, and what to do next
================================================================

Read this to know what is solved, what is not, and what to do about it.
`flight-log.txt` has the session-by-session readings this is drawn from.

Last updated from hardware: 2026-09-22.


WHERE WE ARE IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Sideways control is better and the operator confirms it. Height is not: let
go after a climb and the drone sinks and rises continually. The cause is
measured and it is not the control law — all three flights on 2026-09-22 ran
UNASSISTED, holding height on the BAROMETER, which wandered 72 cm while our
height target sat frozen. The position work built in PRs #25 and #26 has
never run once. The single action that matters most is rebuilding the desktop
sidecar so the app can fly assisted on the one base station the cage has.


THE MEASUREMENT THAT EXPLAINS THE HEIGHT PROBLEM

14.7 s of trace_cc73c0ef.csv, hands off the keys, drone in the air:

  our height target      0.371 m       FROZEN for the whole 14.7 s
  barometer z            -0.07..+0.65  a 72 cm range
  motors                 0 .. 65535    repeatedly, full off to full on

The agent commanded one steady height and never moved it. The barometer
reported the drone swinging through 72 cm. The firmware believed the
barometer, saw a 50 cm error that did not exist, and answered with everything
it had — then cut to zero. The drone physically porpoised chasing sensor noise.

For comparison, the SAME DRONE on the Lighthouse, one base station, an hour
earlier: steady to 1.3 cm. That is 55x better. The fix for height is not
gains; it is flying on the sensor that works.

(The docs previously said an indoor barometer wanders "tens of centimetres".
Measured here: 72 cm. Worse than written.)


WHY EACH REPORTED BEHAVIOUR HAPPENS

Symptom, cause, the evidence, and whether it is fixed. Nothing here is
inferred from how quadrotors usually behave; each cause is a measurement.

1. "IT HOVERS BUT LOWERS AND RISES CONTINUALLY, AND WILL NOT LOCK A HEIGHT"
   CAUSE    The barometer, not the control law.
   EVIDENCE Our height target sat frozen at 0.371 m for 14.7 s while the
            barometer reported a 72 cm swing. The firmware believed it.
            Across that flight the controller demanded MORE THAN FULL
            THROTTLE in 46% of samples (peak 541,901 against an actuator
            maximum of 65,535 — eight times over) and NEGATIVE thrust in
            12%. Motors sat at exactly 0 for 17% of airborne samples.
   MEANING  The controller spent 58% of the flight demanding something
            physically impossible, because it was correcting an error that
            did not exist. The porpoising is the airframe following a
            fictional altitude. Motors at zero in the air is free fall.
   FIXED?   NO — but the cause is not in our code. Fly assisted and z comes
            from the Lighthouse, measured at 1.3 cm against the barometer's
            72 cm.

2. "SIDEWAYS IS A LITTLE BETTER TODAY"
   CAUSE    The easing, not the axis locking.
   EVIDENCE The flight ran the barometer law, where the arrows tilt the
            drone. What improved it is the eased tilt and yaw rates
            (TILT_RATE_DEG_S, YAW_ACCEL_DEG_S2) from the earlier glide work.
   MEANING  A real improvement, and worth keeping — but NOT evidence about
            PR #26. The commanded point never ran. Judgement on the new law
            has to wait for an assisted flight.
   FIXED?   Partly, and by something other than what was assumed.

3. "IT GOES SIDEWAYS, HOPS, AND CRASHES" (the original complaint)
   CAUSE    Three, compounding.
            (a) Velocity-only control. A speed never says WHERE, so tilt
                error integrated into position with nothing observing
                position. A quadrotor is underactuated — it moves sideways
                by tilting — so one degree of unnoticed tilt is 0.17 m/s^2,
                34 cm of drift in two seconds.
            (b) stabilizer.controller = 2 (Mellinger), which ignores every
                gain in flight/tuning.py.
            (c) Stale geometry: 21 cm estimate jumps with the drone still.
   FIXED?   (a) shipped, never flown. (b) pinned to PID before every flight.
            (c) tool written, never run against hardware.

4. "FLIGHTS LAST SECONDS; MY TEAM GOT NINE MINUTES"
   CAUSE    Tumbles, not battery. Session history: aborted-tumbled at 11.6,
            11.1, 15.7, 20.3 and 8.2 seconds.
   FIXED?   Addressed at the root by 3 above, not yet demonstrated.

5. "IT WILL NOT ARM AT 4.15 V"
   CAUSE    A latched crash. supervisor.info = 644, IS_CRASHED set. Battery
            4.13 V, pm.state 0 (normal), roll and pitch under one degree.
            Nothing to do with power.
   FIXED?   YES. One recovery request cleared it; the message now names the
            real reason.

6. "THE DESKTOP SAYS I NEED TWO BASE STATIONS"
   CAUSE    The app runs a FROZEN copy of the agent, built before the change.
   FIXED?   In the agent, yes. In the app, only after a rebuild.

7. "IT FLEW UP BEAUTIFULLY, THEN FELL STRAIGHT DOWN WHEN I LET GO"
   CAUSE    Not the release, and not a fall. The base station dropped out for
            about half a second at that moment and the guard landed it.
   EVIDENCE trace_0f947214.csv. The climb held x and y to between 0.0006 and
            0.017 m, thrust steady near 56,000, no saturation — the first
            flight of the commanded point, and it worked. At t=5.1 s
            lighthouse.bsReceive went to 0 and the key was released; at 5.5 s
            the state went to landing; at 5.7 s the beams were BACK and
            stayed. Descent 1.03 m to 0.37 m over 2.1 s, about 0.31 m/s,
            motors steady near 50,000 — a controlled landing, not a drop. The
            operator pressed up at 7.8 s and nothing happened: landing
            ignores the keys.
   MEANING  The guard aborted on RECEPTION, which is a proxy. What matters is
            whether the ESTIMATE is still good, and it was.
   FIXED?   YES. A blackout is now ridden out while the filter's own variance
            stands behind its position (up to 3 s), and ended inside 0.5 s
            when the variance is growing instead.


MANUAL IS THE FOUNDATION FOR AUTONOMOUS

This is why the architecture matters more than the symptom list. The
commanded point is not a manual-flight trick: a waypoint mission IS the same
machinery with a different source of intent. Manual moves the point with
keys; autonomous moves it along a plan. Same seeding, same leash, same
estimator gates, same guards, same trace.

Two consequences worth holding on to:

- Anything hacked into the manual law to paper over a sensor fault will be
  inherited by every autonomous flight, where nobody is watching to correct
  it. That is the strongest argument against tuning height gains to mask
  barometer noise.

- The quality bar for manual is therefore the quality bar for the whole
  system. Getting a clean, measured hover is not a milestone on the way to
  the real work; it IS the real work, and the mission layer is a thin thing
  on top of it.


WHAT IS DONE — and whether it has actually flown

  PROVEN ON HARDWARE
  - One base station accepted, and it is enough. Checks pass, position
    settles to 1.3 cm. PR #27.
  - Crash recovery. The drone had latched IS_CRASHED; one recovery request
    cleared it in under a second.
  - Honest refusal messages. `sys.canfly = 0` no longer reads as "flat
    battery" — it names what the firmware says. This cost a lab hour before
    it was fixed: a 4.15 V cell, nearly full, sent to the charger.
  - The estimator's own confidence is readable and real: varPX 0.0001 m^2,
    about 1 cm.
  - Motors, battery, telemetry, deck detection.

  SHIPPED, NEVER FLOWN — this is the honest gap
  - The commanded point: every axis you are not pressing is locked. PR #26.
  - Auto-correction to that point, continuously, moving or still.
  - Multi-key rules: opposite pairs cancel and HOLD; different axes compose.
  - The 30 cm leash.
  - Estimator-jump rejection at 2 m/s, and the 10 cm variance gate.
  - Single-station geometry with mirror disambiguation. PR #27.
  All of it is tested — 469 tests — and none of it has met a drone.


WHAT IS NOT FIXED

1. HEIGHT DOES NOT HOLD.   Cause measured (above): barometer noise, not the
   control law. Not yet retested on the Lighthouse, which is the actual fix.

2. THE DESKTOP APP RUNS STALE CODE.  It bundles a FROZEN copy of the agent
   (`externalBin`), built before today. That copy still demands two base
   stations, so the app refuses assisted flight, offers "fly on the
   barometer", and the operator takes it — which is how every flight today
   ended up on the worst available sensor. THIS IS THE ROOT CAUSE OF THE
   HEIGHT PROBLEM, one level up.

3. GEOMETRY HAS NEVER RUN AGAINST HARDWARE.  Until it does, directions may be
   rotated or mirrored: "forward" is not guaranteed to be where the nose
   points. Hover and up/down are unaffected. Arrow tests are not meaningful.

4. drift_m AND THE COMMANDED POINT ARE INVISIBLE.  They go to the trace CSV
   and the agent log. Nothing shows the operator that the drone is 15 cm off
   the spot and correcting.

5. FIRMWARE IS OLD.  CRTP protocol 10; cflib wants 12 and falls back to
   legacy supervisor handling. Not blocking, but a standing suspect.


WHAT NEEDS IMPROVEMENT

- ONE STATION HAS NO REDUNDANCY. It works, and it is measurably good, but a
  single viewpoint means an occluded deck has nowhere else to look — and this
  drone carries no flow deck and no rangefinder (deck.bcFlow2 = 0,
  deck.bcZRanger2 = 0, read off the drone). Lose sight of the station and x
  and y become the accelerometer integrating, measured diverging at about
  1 m/s to 18 m in seconds. A second station is the single biggest robustness
  win available and costs no code.

- THE BAROMETER LAW SHOULD BE HARDER TO REACH BY ACCIDENT. Today it was the
  default outcome of a stale binary. It should be a deliberate choice that
  states what it costs, not the path of least resistance.

- MOTORS REACHING EXACTLY 0 IN FLIGHT is momentary free fall — 17% of
  airborne samples on 2026-09-22. A floor under commanded thrust while
  airborne is worth considering, but ONLY after the estimate is fixed.
  Applied now it would mask the real fault, and per the note above, a mask
  in the manual law is inherited by every autonomous flight.

- CONTROLLER SATURATION IS NOT MEASURED ANYWHERE. 46% of samples demanded
  above the actuator maximum and 12% demanded negative thrust, and nothing
  in the system noticed or said so. A controller spending most of a flight
  asking for the impossible is a loud signal that something upstream is
  wrong, and it is currently only visible by reading a CSV afterwards.


WHAT NEEDS REDIRECTION — where the instructions were wrong

- "TUNE THE HEIGHT GAINS." Wrong target. The trace shows a frozen command and
  a lying sensor. Tuning a controller against a bad estimate hides the fault
  and makes the next one harder to find. Fix the estimate first, then measure
  again before touching a gain.

- "THE BATTERY THRESHOLD IS TOO HIGH." It never was. Both times flight was
  refused this week the battery was healthy — 4.15 V on 2026-09-22, and a
  latched crash was the real reason. Read the reason, never assume it.

- "WE DO NOT NEED THE LIGHTHOUSE TO FLY." True, and it is exactly what went
  wrong today: flying without it is what produced the porpoising. The
  Lighthouse is not a nice-to-have on this airframe, it is the only sensor
  that measures position or a trustworthy height.

- TEST ASSISTED BEFORE JUDGING THE NEW CONTROL LAW. Every impression of the
  drone so far — including "sideways is better" — comes from the barometer
  law. The axis-locking work has not been seen yet, well or badly.

INVARIANTS THIS SESSION ADDED

- The desktop app runs a FROZEN agent. Changing Python does nothing to the
  installed app until build_sidecar.sh re-freezes it. An agent fix that is
  not rebuilt is an agent fix the operator never receives, and the symptom is
  the app behaving like an older version of itself — which reads as a
  hardware fault and is not one.

- Read the flight trace before theorising about flight behaviour. The height
  fault was diagnosed in one pass from columns that were already being
  recorded: a frozen command beside a 72 cm sensor swing. Every guess made
  before reading it was wrong.

- Check WHICH CONTROL LAW flew before drawing any conclusion from a flight.
  The tuning column names it: a barometer profile (velCtlPid.vzKp lowered)
  means unassisted, and nothing about the position law can be concluded from
  that flight at all.
