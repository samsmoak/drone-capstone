1. WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The owner's ask: not every change — a BLOCK of time that departs from what is
normal. Each signal is compared with what it should have read; the departure
is split into blocks of constant level; a block far enough from expected is an
EVENT, with where, when, how much and how sure.

3. DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• EXPECTED IS NOT A CONSTANT. The barometer sits on the drone's board, which
  cools in the propellers' air from takeoff (34.7 → 29.6 °C in 48 s on a
  normal mission). Temperature: raw_temp against a fitted first-order cooling
  curve (robust — Huber weights at the data's own noise, so an event cannot
  bend the curve). corrected_temp is not used: it steps with the engine.
• PRESSURE IS CORRECTED FOR HEIGHT BY PHYSICS (+ ρ·g·z/100), never by a fit
  that would absorb an event at one height; expected is its slow drift. With
  no measured height it is used uncorrected and the track says so.
• BLOCKS BY PELT (Killick 2012), exact, written in numpy — identical to the
  ruptures package on 500 of 500 series; ruptures is a test dependency only.
• AN EVENT needs BOTH 4 × the reading-to-reading noise AND a physical minimum
  — 0.8 °C, 0.2 hPa — just above the most any normal flight's blocks wander
  (0.781 °C, 0.173 hPa). CHANGED 2026-10-09 from 4 × the residual's own MAD:
  in a short flight a big event IS most of the residual and inflated the
  threshold that judged it (5 °C patches found only 65 %).
• ADJACENT DEPARTING BLOCKS GOING THE SAME WAY ARE ONE EVENT (a ramp is a
  staircase of blocks), carrying its mean departure and its PEAK.
• NO ML MODEL FOR THE SENSORS: no labelled runs exist; the measured blocks
  are explainable and need none.
• WHAT "FAULTY" MEANS FOR AN IMAGE (story 4.4; set 2026-10-10, PROVISIONAL —
  subject to change): EITHER of two things, both visible in a 324 × 244
  grayscale frame:
    - a gauge's needle in the HIGH zone (the dark band at the top of the dial;
      the pressure gauge reads 0–600 kPa). The needle in the middle of the
      dial is safe, however close it gets to a band's edge — the low band is
      not a fault.
    - VISIBLE DAMAGE to the test equipment.
  Not heat — the camera cannot see it, and the thermal "faulty" is
  blocks@1's (scene.txt). Story 6.1 (Hannah and Yordanos) owes the team's own
  written definition per test box; when it lands, it replaces this, and the
  dataset (story 4.1) is labelled to match. If the definition changes,
  relabel — a model trained on the old meaning is wrong, not merely stale.
  defect_type in ml/image-classifier/manifests/images.csv names which kind.
• THE IMAGE MODEL IS AN ONNX FILE, LISTED IN pipeline/models/MODELS.json WITH
  "stage": "classify.image" ("frames": "enhanced" if trained on clahe@1's
  copy; "threshold" for p_faulty). Input: the camera's 324 × 244 gray frame,
  squashed to 224 × 224, normalised, repeated to 3 channels — held identical
  to ml/image-classifier/preprocess.py by a test. Output: 1 × 2 logits.
  No entry, no model: frames stay "unknown". A model that will not load (file
  missing, sha256 changed, wrong shape) costs the frames their labels, with
  the reason, and never the sensors' analysis. The classifier's version then
  reads "1+<model>@<version>", so a result says which model judged it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INVARIANTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• On 115 normal flights: no event. Planted warm patches of 2 °C or more:
  always found; 1 °C: 97 %. Pressure steps of 0.3 hPa: always found.
• A stretch shorter than a minimum block (2 s) between gaps or flags is not
  analysed; the first 3 s after takeoff are left out.
• Flagged values never reach a model; °F gives the same events, deltas × 9/5.
• An event that fills most of a short flight cannot be told from that
  flight's baseline — calibration plants only in flights a minute or more in
  the air.
