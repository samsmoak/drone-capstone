--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Story 4.6, "define normal thresholds". The classifier's minimums and the
interpreter's severity factors were measured on flights with nothing there.
This measures what a REAL heat source does to the sensor and recommends the
constants, with the measurements behind them.

THE LAB PROCEDURE
  1. Put a hand warmer (or any steady heat source) at one inspection point of
     a mission, at the height the drone holds there. Note the point.
  2. Fly the mission with Process flights (DPP) on. Repeat — two or three
     flights, the warmer at different points if you can.
  3. On the laptop:
       cropwatcher calibrate --flight <id> --at P2 [--flight <id> --at P3 …]
  4. Read the table: what the sensor saw at each marked point (°C above its
     cooling curve, and in noise units), whether today's settings find it and
     how severe they call it, and how far the rest of those flights wandered.
  5. If it recommends a change, make it in a reviewed pull request, citing the
     saved report in ml/anomaly-eval/MEASUREMENTS.txt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• IT NEVER EDITS THE CODE. A threshold is a reviewed change; the report is the
  evidence for it.
• RECOMMENDATIONS FOLLOW TWO RULES: the minimum stays above the most a block
  wanders with nothing there; the weakest real heat source should read at
  least "warning" (WARNING_FACTOR is lowered to make it so, never below 1.1).
• A heat source too weak to tell apart from a normal flight is SAID — move it
  closer or use a warmer one — rather than lowering the minimum to it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INVARIANTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• One --at per --flight, in order; a point the flight did not fly is refused.
